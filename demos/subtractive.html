How Subtractive Synthesis Works

Subtractive synthesis starts with a harmonically rich waveform — like a sawtooth or square wave — and uses filters to remove (subtract) portions of the spectrum to sculpt the timbre.

Signal path: Sawtooth Oscillator (110 Hz) ↓ Low-Pass Filter ← cutoff, resonance ↓ Amplifier ← volume ↓ Output

A low-pass filter lets frequencies below the cutoff pass through and attenuates everything above. Raising the resonance (Q) boosts a narrow band around the cutoff, creating the characteristic synth "whistle."

The orange curve overlaid on the spectrum shows the theoretical filter response — watch it move as you sweep left/right and tilt up/down.
