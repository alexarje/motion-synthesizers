How Additive Synthesis Works

Additive synthesis is based on the Fourier theorem: any periodic sound can be recreated by summing enough sine waves (called partials or harmonics).

output(t) = Σ Aₙ · sin(2π · n · f₀ · t) n=1…N where f₀ = fundamental frequency Aₙ = amplitude of the n-th harmonic

Moving left/right morphs between different amplitude envelopes (profiles): a pure sine (only harmonic 1), triangle (odd harmonics ∝ 1/n²), square (odd harmonics ∝ 1/n), and sawtooth (all harmonics ∝ 1/n).

Moving up/down changes the fundamental frequency from 110 Hz (low A) to 880 Hz (high A). Moving creates volume — stay still and it fades to silence.
